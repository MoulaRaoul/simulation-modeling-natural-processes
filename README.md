# Simulation and Modeling of Natural Processes

Personal study repository dedicated to **simulation, mathematical modeling, numerical methods, and computational experiments in Python**.

This repository follows my study of the course *Simulation and Modeling of Natural Processes* and is intended to become a structured scientific notebook rather than a simple collection of course exercises.

## Purpose

The main goals of this repository are to:

- document the essential concepts of mathematical modeling and simulation;
- implement numerical methods from scratch when appropriate;
- reproduce and extend practical exercises through personal experiments;
- study the assumptions, limitations, stability, accuracy, and computational cost of models;
- develop clean and reproducible Python simulations;
- build a progressive portfolio in computational science.

## Topics

The study will progressively cover:

1. Modeling and simulation fundamentals
2. Monte Carlo methods
3. High-performance computing concepts and Python
4. Dynamical systems and numerical integration
5. Cellular automata
6. Lattice Boltzmann methods and computational fluid dynamics
7. Particle systems and the N-body problem
8. Discrete-event simulation
9. Agent-based modeling

## Working method

For each important topic, the workflow will be:

1. Understand the physical or natural phenomenon.
2. Identify the modeling assumptions.
3. Formulate the mathematical or computational model.
4. Implement the model in Python.
5. Verify the numerical behavior.
6. Visualize and interpret the results.
7. Modify parameters or assumptions and perform additional experiments.
8. Record conclusions, limitations, and possible extensions.

## Scientific practice

The emphasis is not only on obtaining numerical results, but also on understanding:

- why a particular model is appropriate;
- what assumptions are being made;
- how space and time are represented;
- how numerical approximations introduce error;
- whether a simulation is stable and reproducible;
- how computational cost influences modeling choices;
- and how simulation results should be interpreted in relation to the real system.

## Academic integrity

This repository contains personal notes, original implementations, and independent numerical experiments. It is not intended to reproduce proprietary course materials or official assessment solutions.
